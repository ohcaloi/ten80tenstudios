VA vs. system + specialist: why renting hours keeps failing

A VA agency sells you hours. Nobody sells you the workflow — that part stays your job. And it's the part that was killing you.

Hiring a virtual assistant sounds like the fix. Then you spend month one writing SOPs, month two explaining your tools, and month three interviewing replacements. The problem isn't the assistant. It's the model.

What you're actually buying

A traditional VA agency sells a block of hours. A person shows up in your inbox, willing and capable — and waits for direction. What happens in those hours, how tasks flow, which tools get used, what "done" looks like: all of that is still yours to invent, document and police.

You wanted to buy an outcome. You bought a seat.

The hidden job you just took on

The moment the VA starts, you inherit three unpaid roles. Workflow designer — mapping how the work should move, because nobody else will. Trainer — walking through every tool and edge case. Quality controller — checking output until trust builds. Founders routinely spend more hours managing the VA in the first months than the VA saves them.

That's the quiet reason so many VA experiments end in "it was easier to do it myself."

Turnover resets everything

Then the reset button. VAs move on — it's a high-churn industry — and when they do, the process knowledge lives in their head, not in a system. New VA, same SOPs to re-explain, same trust to rebuild, same three months. Renting hours means renting memory, and the lease keeps expiring.

The System + Specialist difference

We built Ten80Ten to sell the thing you actually wanted: the outcome. The workflow is our job, the software is custom, and the person is trained on the system before they're trained on you:

  • We design the workflow — you never have to direct anyone
  • Custom automation does the repetitive 80% before a human touches it
  • Your specialist runs the system day-to-day and owns the result
  • 6-month Perfect Hire Guarantee — if it's not a fit, we replace them free while the system keeps running
  • You own the code, hosting and process docs. No lock-in, ever.

Notice the turnover math changes completely. Because the process lives in the system — not in someone's head — a specialist change is a handover, not a restart.

Software a rent-a-VA shop can't give you

There's a second difference that gets missed. VA agencies staff people; they don't build software. So the ceiling of what a rented VA can do is the ceiling of your current tools. If your follow-up lives in sticky notes and spreadsheets, that's what your VA inherits.

We build the plumbing first — portals, dashboards, integrations that connect the tools you already use. The specialist then works on top of software shaped to the job. That's why the same person gets dramatically more done: the boring 80% is already handled by the time they open their laptop, and their hours go to the work that actually needs a human.

What it costs, honestly

A system + specialist retainer anchors at $1,495 a month, month-to-month. Compare that with a mid-range VA at 20–30 hours a week plus the 5–10 hours of your own time spent directing them — priced at whatever your hour is worth. Focused system builds start at $3,000 one-time, with a 30-day warranty and the first automation live in about two weeks.

Which one do you need?

If your processes are already documented, your tools already talk to each other, and you enjoy managing — a rented VA can work. If what you want is your 35 hours a month back without becoming an operations manager, buy the outcome, not the hours.

We'll show you exactly which of your tasks fit the model — before you spend anything.
